Overview - Model Type
Model Architecture:
Ensemble Machine Learning Models for Agricultural Yield Prediction:
  • Linear Regression - Baseline interpretable model
  • Ridge Regression - L2 regularization to prevent overfitting
  • Lasso Regression - L1 regularization with feature selection
  • Random Forest - Ensemble decision trees for non-linear relationships
  • XGBoost - Gradient boosting for advanced pattern recognition
Input Features:
Environmental and agricultural variables:
  • Seasonal rainfall metrics (sum, mean, totals)
  • Temperature data (Tmax, Tmin, Tmean)
  • Humidity measurements
  • Cultivation area (hectares)
  • Growing degree days
  • Meteorological indices
Output:
Weather-based decision support (NOT yield predictions due to negative R² performance)
• Real-time weather forecasts (7-10 day horizon)
• Agronomic best practices
• Extreme weather alerts
• Tactical decision guidance
Primary Intended Users
  • Smallholder Farmers in Dang Deukhuri region seeking weather information and agricultural guidance
  • Agricultural Extension Workers providing advisory services to multiple farmers
  • Policy Makers at district and national levels for resource allocation planning
  • Researchers studying climate-agriculture relationships in Nepal
Primary Intended Uses
  • Providing accurate short-term weather forecasts (7-10 days) for tactical farming decisions
  • Delivering agronomic guidance tailored to current weather conditions
  • Issuing extreme weather alerts (heavy rainfall, heat waves, droughts)
  • Supporting irrigation scheduling based on forecast dry spells
  • Optimizing pesticide/fungicide application timing during rain-free windows
  • Enabling harvest acceleration before predicted storms
  • Providing historical climate context for comparative assessment
Out of Scope Uses
⚠️ CRITICAL: The following uses are EXPLICITLY OUT OF SCOPE due to model limitations:
  • ❌ Seasonal yield prediction - All models achieved negative R² values (-13 to -22), indicating predictions worse than baseline mean
  • ❌ Long-term climate forecasting (>10 days) - Beyond scientifically validated forecast skill horizons
  • ❌ Financial decision-making based on yield predictions - Unreliable predictions could cause severe economic harm
  • ❌ Credit/loan approval based on predicted yields - Data quality insufficient for high-stakes decisions
  • ❌ Insurance premium calculation - Model uncertainty too high for actuarial applications
  • ❌ Land valuation based on predicted agricultural productivity
  • ❌ Deployment in regions outside Dang Deukhuri without retraining and validation
Limitations
1. Data Quality Limitations:
  • Monthly vs. Daily Data Paradox: DHM maintains only monthly climate records. Monthly aggregates cannot capture daily weather variations critical for rice cultivation (e.g., consecutive-day rainfall for transplanting, single-week temperature extremes during flowering)
  • Extensive Missing Values: 30-40% of historical weather data contains NA entries requiring statistical imputation (forward-fill, mean imputation), introducing systematic bias
  • Prohibitive Cost of Daily Data: Obtaining 35 years of daily weather data from DHM proved financially impossible for student research
2. Infrastructure Limitations:
  • Inadequate Weather Station Density: Nearest comprehensive stations operate 20-50km from agricultural areas, missing localized microclimate variations
  • Equipment Malfunctions: Rural stations lack automated recording systems, resulting in incomplete time series
3. Model Performance Limitations:

4. Missing Critical Variables:
  • Farmer Management Practices: No data on variety selection, fertilizer timing, irrigation scheduling, pest control - factors accounting for 30-40% of yield variance
  • Soil Data Coverage: Limited spatial resolution of soil surveys
5. Climate Regime Shift:
Models trained on 1990-2017 data failed on 2018-2024 test data due to increased climate anomalies (delayed monsoons, temperature variability, extreme rainfall), indicating non-stationary climate-yield relationships.
Factors That Can Affect Classifications/Predictions
  • Temporal aggregation level: Monthly data masks daily extremes
  • Imputation methodology: Different imputation methods produce different results
  • Climate non-stationarity: Historical patterns don't generalize to climate-shifted future
  • Spatial mismatches: Weather stations 20-50km from fields
  • Missing management data: Unobserved farmer decisions
  • Monsoon variability: Unpredictable monsoon onset/withdrawal timing
Metrics
Model Evaluation Metrics:
  • R² (Coefficient of Determination): Measures proportion of variance explained. Negative values indicate performance worse than predicting mean.
  • RMSE (Root Mean Squared Error): Average prediction error in MT/HA
  • MAE (Mean Absolute Error): Average absolute prediction error
  • Cross-Validation R²: Time-series CV respecting chronological ordering

Training and Evaluation Data
Data Sources:
  • Department of Hydrology and Meteorology (DHM): Monthly rainfall, temperature, humidity data (1990-2025)
  • Nepal Agricultural Research Council (NARC): Soil test results, historical yield data
  • Ministry of Agriculture: Aggregated government statistics
  • Sentinel-2: Satellite vegetation indices
  • CHIRPS: Climate Hazards Group InfraRed Precipitation with Station data
Temporal Split:
• Training: 1990-2017 (27 years)
• Testing: 2018-2024 (6 years, held-out)
Data Quality Issues:
• 30-40% missing values in temperature, humidity, wind speed
• Spatial gaps in soil coverage
• No farmer management practice data
• Monthly temporal resolution (insufficient for agriculture)
Quantitative Analysis
Key Finding: All models achieved negative R² values on test data, definitively rejecting Hypothesis 1 that ML could improve yield predictions under current data constraints.
Cross-Validation Results:
• Linear Regression CV: RMSE = 2.39 ± 2.65, R² = -873
• Ridge CV: RMSE = 0.76 ± 0.41, R² = -36.71
• Random Forest CV: RMSE = 0.84 ± 0.22, R² = -28.46
• XGBoost CV: RMSE = 0.80 ± 0.27, R² = -25.14
Conclusion:
Under current data constraints, no optimal strategy exists for reliable rice yield prediction. Studies achieving R² of 0.70-0.85 utilize daily weather records, satellite imagery, comprehensive soil surveys, and farmer management data - none of which were available for this research.
Ethical Considerations
1. Responsible Non-Deployment:
Most significant ethical success: Deliberate decision to NOT deploy yield prediction functionality despite substantial development effort. With R² values of -13 to -22, deploying models would be fundamentally irresponsible and potentially harmful to farmers.
2. Transparency:
All negative R² values documented openly. System interface communicates uncertainty explicitly. No concealment of poor performance.
3. User Autonomy:
Recommendations framed as suggestions ("consider", "may help") rather than prescriptive directives ("you should"). Preserves farmer decision-making authority.
4. Privacy Protection:
Weather data from public APIs requires no personal farmer information. Yield data from aggregated government statistics protects privacy.
5. Digital Divide Challenges:
  • Requires internet connectivity (4G inconsistent in rural Dang)
  • Smartphone prerequisite excludes some farmers
  • Disproportionately serves wealthier, younger, digitally connected farmers
6. Data Inequities:
Training data reflects farmers with documentation/land tenure. Marginalized farmers (landless laborers, tenant farmers, women) invisible in official statistics.
